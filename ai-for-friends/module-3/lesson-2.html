What Is a Claude Project?

A Project is a reusable workspace on claude.ai that already knows your role, your rules, and your reference documents.

10 MIN REQUIRED
1. Three Parts 2. Why It Beats a Blank Chat 3. Your First Project

By the end of this lesson, you will understand what a Claude Project is, name its three parts, and decide what recurring work your first Project should cover. A Project is a dedicated workspace you set up once and reuse in every chat.

A plain chat starts from a blank slate every time. You re-explain your job, re-paste your templates, and re-state your rules on each new conversation. A Project ends that repetition — it holds your standing instructions and your reference documents in one place, so every chat inside it gets to work immediately.

1The Three Parts of a Project

A Project is a dedicated space on claude.ai built around one kind of recurring work. It has three parts: a name, custom instructions, and Project knowledge. Learn what each part does and you understand the whole tool.

PartWhat It IsWhat It Does For You
NameA short label for what this Project is for (e.g., "Recruiting Assistant")Helps you find the right Project in your sidebar
Custom instructionsPlain-language standing rules that tell Claude who it is, how to behave, and how to format answersWritten once, they apply to every chat inside the Project
Project knowledgeReference files you upload (templates, style guides, policies, past examples)Claude can draw on them in any conversation without you re-attaching
The Shape of a Project

A Project is a folder-shaped workspace inside claude.ai. You set it up once — what it is for, how it should behave, what documents it should know about — and every chat you start inside it inherits all of that automatically.

The Two That Matter

The custom instructions are the standing orders. The Project knowledge is the reference shelf. Together they mean you stop repeating yourself.

2Why a Project Beats a Blank Chat

A plain chat forgets you the moment it ends. A Project remembers. Understanding the difference tells you when a Project is worth setting up and when a quick chat is enough. Think of it as the difference between calling a random person for help and calling YOUR assistant — the random person needs the whole backstory every time, but your assistant already knows your role and gets straight to work.

Every new plain chat:

  • You re-explain your role from scratch.
  • You re-paste your templates and examples.
  • You re-state your formatting rules.

Every chat inside a Project:

  • Your instructions are already in effect.
  • Your documents are already available.
  • You have one dedicated space for this work.
The Test

If you find yourself pasting the same context into a chat twice, that context belongs in a Project. Set it up once and stop repeating yourself.

3Decide What Your First Project Is For

A Project works best when it covers one kind of recurring work. Before you build anything, decide what that work is. The examples below show how five roles scope a first Project.

RoleProject NameThe Recurring Work It Covers
LegalContract ReviewClause review, risk flagging, tracking defined terms
MarketingContent AssistantRepurposing content, enforcing brand voice, campaign summaries
Industrial EngineeringProcess & Work-Order AssistantNonconformance summaries, standard-work drafting, work-order status tracking
International RelationsDiplomatic Affairs AssistantBriefing memos, protocol checks, agreement and cable tracking
HR (Recruiting)Recruiting AssistantCandidate summaries, outreach messages, pipeline tracking
One Kind of Work Per Project

Keep each Project to a single kind of recurring work. A Project that tries to do everything follows no clear set of rules. When you have a second kind of work, build a second Project.

Name It After the Work

Name your Project after the recurring task, not after yourself. "Contract Review" tells you what belongs inside it. "My Project" does not.

Verification

You can name the three parts of a Project (name, custom instructions, Project knowledge)
You can explain in one sentence why a Project beats a plain chat
You have chosen the recurring work your first Project will cover
Not Working?

If any check feels unclear, revisit the step where that concept appeared. The Key Concepts table in Step 1 lists the three parts, and Step 3 shows how five roles scope their first Project.
